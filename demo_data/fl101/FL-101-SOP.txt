1. PURPOSE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

This SOP explains how to run the FL-101 Bottle Filler safely and correctly.
It covers the checks before starting, the start-up sequence, the normal
settings, the checks during the shift, what to do for each alarm, bottle
size changeovers, cleaning, records and shutdown.

Following this SOP keeps people safe, keeps every bottle within the
declared fill amount, and keeps glass and other foreign objects out of the
product.

2. SCOPE

This SOP applies to:

  - 500 ml and 330 ml glass bottles
  - Still water only

This SOP does not cover the capper (CP-102) or the labeller (LB-103). They
have their own SOPs. It does not cover repairs: repairs follow the work
instructions listed in Section 5.

Filler operator
  - Runs the machine as described in this SOP
  - Completes the pre-start checks and the checks during the shift
  - Responds to alarms and records them in the shift log
  - Stops the machine if anything looks unsafe

Shift lead
  - Makes sure only trained operators run FL-101
  - Signs the pre-start checklist and the changeover record
  - Calls maintenance or the quality lead when this SOP says so

Quality lead
  - Owns the glass breakage procedure and all QA HOLD decisions
  - Signs off the line before restart after any glass breakage
  - Checks the checkweigher records every day

Maintenance technician
  - Carries out repairs using the work instructions
  - Replaces fill valve seals using WI-FL101-001

Maintenance supervisor
  - Is called for repeated alarms, electrical faults and anything this SOP
    does not cover

4. DEFINITIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  Bowl             The product tank on top of the filler. Water flows from
                   the bowl down through the fill valves.
  Fill valve       One of the 24 valves that fill the bottles. Each valve is
                   numbered 1 to 24.
  Seal kit         The rubber seal and O-ring inside each fill valve
                   (part SEAL-FV-24). A worn seal lets water drip.
  Infeed           Where empty bottles enter the machine.
  Guide rails      The side rails that steer bottles into the infeed. They
                   are set to a different position for each bottle size.
  Star wheel       A toothed wheel that holds each bottle in the right place
                   as it moves through the machine.
  Checkweigher     A scale on the conveyor that weighs every filled bottle and
                   pushes out any that are outside the allowed range.
  Changeover       Switching the machine from one bottle size to another.
  Underfill        A bottle with less than 495 ml.
  Overfill         A bottle with more than 505 ml.
  LOTO             Lockout / tagout. Switching off and locking the power so
                   the machine cannot start while someone works on it.
  QA HOLD          A label that means "do not ship until the quality lead has
                   checked it".
  Sanitation rinse A 10-minute rinse of the bowl, valves and nozzles with
                   clean water.

6. SAFETY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

6.1 PPE required on Filling Line 1
  - Safety glasses at all times
  - Cut-resistant gloves when handling glass or change parts
  - Hearing protection
  - Safety shoes

6.2 Lockout / tagout (LOTO)
LOTO is required before any maintenance, clearing a jam, changing parts, or
reaching into the machine. Follow SAF-003:
  1. Switch off power at the main isolator.
  2. Lock the isolator with your personal padlock.
  3. Tag it with your name and the date.
  4. Try to start the machine to prove it cannot run.
Only the person who fitted a lock may remove it.

6.3 Guards
All guard doors have safety switches. The machine stops if a guard door is
opened. Never bypass or tape over a guard switch.

6.4 Main safety rules
  - Never clear a jam while the machine is running.
  - Never reach past a guard.
  - Never use compressed air near open bottles or broken glass.
  - Keep the floor dry. Report leaks and spills straight away.

7. HOW THE MACHINE WORKS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  1. Empty bottles arrive on the infeed conveyor.
  2. The guide rails steer them into the infeed star wheel.
  3. The star wheel places each bottle under a fill valve on the turning
     carousel.
  4. The fill valve opens for the fill time (3.2 seconds) and fills the
     bottle from the bowl.
  5. The discharge star wheel moves the full bottle out to the capper.
  6. After capping, the checkweigher weighs every bottle and rejects any
     outside 495 to 505 ml.
  7. Good bottles go on to the labeller.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
8. PRE-START CHECKS (complete before every shift)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Record every check on the pre-start checklist. The shift lead signs it.

8.1 Fill valves and seals
  - Look at each of the 24 fill valves for cracks or dripping.
  - A valve that drips between fills has a worn seal. Report it before
    starting.
  - Seal kits (part SEAL-FV-24) are replaced every 3 months or every
    1,500 running hours, whichever comes first.
  - Spare seal kits are kept in the Filling Line 1 spares cabinet, shelf 2.
    The minimum stock is 10 kits.

8.2 Infeed and guide rails
  - Check the guide rails are set for the bottle size being run:
      500 ml bottles: rail position A
      330 ml bottles: rail position B
  - Check the rails are locked and the matching star wheel is fitted.

8.3 Product supply
  - Check the bowl level indicator shows NORMAL.
  - Check the product supply pressure is 1.5 to 2.0 bar.
  - The supply filter (part FLT-PS-10) is cleaned once a week. Check the
    date on the filter tag.

8.4 Checkweigher
  - Pass the three test bottles through the checkweigher: 490 ml, 500 ml
    and 510 ml.
  - The 490 ml and 510 ml bottles must be rejected. The 500 ml bottle must
    pass. If not, do not start: call the quality lead.

8.5 Glass and guards
  - Check the red glass kit is on its hook: brush, dustpan, sealed bin and
    QA HOLD labels.
  - Walk round the machine and check there is no broken glass.
  - Check every guard door is closed.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
9. START-UP SEQUENCE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  1. Complete the pre-start checks (Section 8).
  2. Open the product supply valve and let the bowl fill to NORMAL.
  3. Run the sanitation rinse (10 minutes) if the machine has been stopped
     for more than 4 hours.
  4. Start at 60 bottles per minute.
  5. Weigh the first 20 bottles. All must be 495 to 505 ml.
  6. Raise the speed to 120 bottles per minute.
  7. Record the start time in the shift log.

10. OPERATING SETTINGS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  Bottle:                  500 ml glass (or 330 ml after a changeover)
  Target fill:             500 ml
  Allowed fill range:      495 to 505 ml (checkweigher rejects outside this)
  Line speed:              120 bottles per minute (maximum 130)
  Product supply pressure: 1.5 to 2.0 bar
  Low pressure alarm:      below 1.3 bar
  Fill time:               3.2 seconds, factory set

The fill time must NOT be changed by operators. Changing it needs written
approval from engineering. A longer fill time does not fix underfill: it
hides the real cause and makes bottles overflow (see NCR-2026-012).

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
11. CHECKS DURING THE SHIFT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  Every hour
    - Take 5 bottles off the line and weigh them by hand. Record the
      results. All must be 495 to 505 ml.
    - Record the checkweigher reject count.

  Every 2 hours
    - Look at the fill valves for dripping. Report any dripping valve to
      the shift lead.

  At the start, middle and end of the shift
    - Walk round the machine and check for broken glass. Sign the glass
      check sheet.

If the checkweigher rejects more than 1 bottle in 100 over an hour, tell
the shift lead. A rising reject count is often the first sign of worn seals.

12. ALARM RESPONSE PROCEDURES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

12.1 Underfill
Alarm message: "Fill level low — bottles under target volume"
The alarm starts when the checkweigher rejects 3 underfilled bottles in a row.

This is the most common alarm on FL-101. Causes, most common first:
  1. Worn fill valve seal — the valve drips between fills
  2. Blocked fill nozzle — debris in the nozzle, no dripping
  3. Low product supply pressure — see Section 12.4

Immediate action:
  Step 1 — Press STOP (normal stop, not the emergency stop unless someone
           is in danger).
  Step 2 — Read the checkweigher report. It shows which valve numbers are
           underfilling.
  Step 3 — Look at those valves. Dripping means a worn seal: call the
           maintenance technician to replace the seal kit using work
           instruction WI-FL101-001.
  Step 4 — No dripping: lock out the machine (Section 6.2) and clean the
           nozzle of that valve.
  Step 5 — Check the product supply pressure (Section 12.4).

Do NOT increase the fill time to make up for underfill. It hides worn
seals and causes overflow and spillage (NCR-2026-012).

Three or more underfill alarms in one shift means a systemic fault, usually
several worn seals. Stop production, raise a maintenance work order and
call the maintenance supervisor.

12.2 Infeed Jam
Alarm message: "Infeed jam — bottles stopped at infeed"

Causes, most common first:
  1. Guide rails not reset after a bottle size changeover — check the
     changeover record first if a changeover happened this shift
  2. Chipped or damaged bottles
  3. Bottle feed conveyor running too fast

SAFETY: Never reach into the infeed or the star wheel while the machine is
running. Stop the machine and lock it out (Section 6.2) before clearing
any jam.

Immediate action:
  Step 1 — Press STOP.
  Step 2 — Lock out the machine (Section 6.2).
  Step 3 — Remove the jammed bottles wearing cut-resistant gloves.
  Step 4 — Check the guide rail position matches the bottle size
           (Section 8.2). Reset the rails if they are wrong.
  Step 5 — Remove any chipped or damaged bottles from the infeed.
  Step 6 — Remove the lock, restart at 60 bottles per minute for 2 minutes,
           then return to normal speed.

If a bottle breaks during a jam, stop and follow Section 12.3.

12.3 Glass Breakage
Alarm message: "Glass breakage detected in filler"

CRITICAL — broken glass can end up inside filled bottles. This is a
product safety event. Follow GBP-001.

Immediate action:
  Step 1 — Stop the line at once with the emergency stop.
  Step 2 — Do not open any guard until the machine has stopped and is
           locked out (Section 6.2).
  Step 3 — Put every bottle filled in the last 30 minutes on hold. Label
           them QA HOLD. Throw away any open bottles within 2 metres of
           the break.
  Step 4 — Clean up with the red glass kit only. Use a vacuum, never
           compressed air (air spreads glass).
  Step 5 — Rinse the filler (sanitation rinse, 10 minutes).
  Step 6 — The quality lead must sign off before the line restarts.
  Step 7 — Record the event in the glass breakage log.

Bottles on QA HOLD may only be released after the quality lead has
inspected them.

12.4 Product Supply Pressure Low
Alarm message: "Product supply pressure low — below 1.3 bar"

Low supply pressure makes bottles underfill.

Causes, most common first:
  1. Supply filter blocked — clean or replace the filter (FLT-PS-10)
  2. Air in the supply line — bleed the air using the bleed valve on the bowl
  3. Supply tank running low — check the tank level with the water
     treatment room

Immediate action:
  Step 1 — Press STOP.
  Step 2 — Clean the supply filter.
  Step 3 — Bleed air from the supply line.
  Step 4 — Confirm the pressure is back to 1.5 to 2.0 bar.
  Step 5 — Restart, then weigh the first 20 bottles. All must be 495 to 505 ml.

12.5 Other Messages
  "Guard door open"            Close the door. The machine will not run with
                               a door open.
  "Checkweigher reject bin full" Empty the bin into the reject cage and record
                               the count.
  "Emergency stop pressed"     Find out why before resetting. Record it.

13. ESCALATION — WHEN TO CALL MAINTENANCE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Call the maintenance supervisor when:
  - The same alarm happens 3 or more times in one shift
  - Underfill continues after the seals have been replaced
  - Any electrical fault code shows on the control panel
  - A guide rail will not lock in position
  - Any glass breakage (also call the quality lead)

Do not attempt electrical repairs. Always call maintenance.

14. BOTTLE SIZE CHANGEOVER
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

When changing between 500 ml and 330 ml bottles:
  1. Lock out the machine (Section 6.2).
  2. Move the guide rails to the position on the changeover sheet CS-FL101
     (A for 500 ml, B for 330 ml) and lock them.
  3. Fit the matching star wheel.
  4. Remove the lock and run 20 bottles at 60 bottles per minute.
  5. Check none jam and all fill correctly, then return to normal speed.
  6. Record the changeover in the shift log. The shift lead signs the
     changeover record.

Most infeed jams in the first hour after a changeover come from rails left
in the wrong position.

15. CLEANING AND HYGIENE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  - Run the sanitation rinse (10 minutes) at the end of every shift.
  - A full deep clean is done every Sunday by the cleaning team (CL-FL101).
  - Only food-grade lubricant (FGL-1) may be used anywhere product can
    touch.
  - Never use compressed air near open bottles.
  - Wash hands before touching fill valves or nozzles.

16. QUALITY RECORDS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  - Hourly fill weights (5 bottles every hour)
  - Checkweigher reject count
  - Glass check sheet (start, middle and end of shift)
  - Shift log: alarms, actions taken, changeovers, stoppages
  - Glass breakage log (only when a breakage happens)

Records are kept for 2 years.

17. END OF SHIFT AND SHUTDOWN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  1. Let the last bottles clear the machine.
  2. Close the product supply valve.
  3. Run the sanitation rinse (10 minutes).
  4. Do the end-of-shift glass check.
  5. Record bottles filled, bottles rejected and any alarms in the shift log.
  6. Report any dripping valves to the next shift.
