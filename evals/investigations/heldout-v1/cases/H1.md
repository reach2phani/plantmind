**In one sentence:** search found everything H1 needed, but PlantMind treated a "don't start, call quality, record it" question as an Overfill repair job. It invented repair steps (one the SOP forbids), copied in unrelated rules, rated it too high and said it was sure. ## Part A. The case

### 1. Industrial context

- An operator is doing the checks before starting Filling Line 1 (FL-101, a bottle filler).
- One check tests the **checkweigher**, the scale that weighs every filled bottle and rejects any outside 495–505 ml.
- The test uses three bottles: 490 ml and 510 ml must be rejected, and 500 ml must pass.
- The 510 ml bottle went through. **The checkweigher failed its test.** Nothing is broken on the filler itself.
- The operator wants a shortcut: start anyway and weigh bottles by hand.

## Part D. Lessons

### 11. Learning takeaway

**An AI system built for breakdowns will turn every question into a breakdown.** PlantMind's fault matcher, its rule list and its report template all assume "something broke, fix it". When the question is different ("a check failed, may I start?"), no part says "this is different". Each part squeezes it into the repair shape, and fills the empty slots with invented content. The right core answer survived only because it was short and repeated in three places.

**Finding the right information does not mean using it.** Search found everything. One fact was lost in a summary (H1-01), and one was present but unused (H1-07).

**The checker is part of the system.** Its safety score said "clean" on an answer with a forbidden instruction.

Step 3: vacuum with the red glass kit. Rating: CRITICAL."*

**Questions:**
1. Which finding type is the CRITICAL rating?
2. Which cause most likely produced Steps 1–3?
3. What would you check in the trace first?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **Wrong rating.** No glass was found; this is a paperwork question.
2. **Wrong problem chosen** (a sheet question matched to Glass Breakage), then **Template pressure** (LOTO / inspect / fix slots filled from the glass procedure).

</details>

### What worked

- **The core safety answer was right** in both runs: don't start, don't hand-weigh, call the quality lead, the 490/500/510 rule.
- **Search found the right SOP section** (8.4 at rank 3) and the checklist line (rank 4).
- **The graph carried the key rule** ("If not, do not start: call the quality lead") as a verified fact, even through the wrong fault.
- **The alarm helper read the log correctly** ("1 occurrence").
- **Cost was within limits:** 15,952 tokens (limit 20,000); about 10.5 s of working time (67 s total, minus 57 s waiting for the free-tier quota).
